About me

Two things you should know about me: I love music, and I love computers.

I got into music production by covering songs I loved. Getting the notes right wasn’t enough, though: I wanted the same sounds, too. Now I write and produce my own indie rock, and I’m just as curious about how a synth or guitar pedal works on the inside as I am about the song it’s used in.

My projects follow a consistent pattern: “learn, create, share.” I learn something out of curiosity, build something I’ll actually use with it, and document it for others to see, usually on my YouTube channel, Dasma Tech. That’s how I ended up repairing my grandparents’ record player, writing a music bot for my friends, and building a synthesizer from scratch.

In high school, I also ran the sound board for our musicals, hosted the weather segment on our morning broadcast as “Avalanche Adams,” and helped design and open the new makerspace in our building.

Now I’m studying electrical engineering at Harvard. Long term, I want to design consumer audio electronics: the instruments and gear musicians use. I’m also considering a secondary in neuroscience, on the computational side. I’ve kept a daily journal since eighth grade, and no amount of text captures what a moment actually felt like, which has made me curious about how the brain stores memories and thoughts.

I’m looking for internships in embedded systems, audio, hardware, and software. Feel free to reach out via email!
