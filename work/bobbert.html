Bobbert

A Discord music bot for listening to music with friends on calls.

When
May 2022 – now
Built with
Python, asyncio, discord.py, yt-dlp, FFmpeg
Links
Code

In 2022, I wrote a Discord bot so my friends and I could listen to music together on calls. Anyone in the voice channel can queue songs, skip, pause, go back, jump to any song in the queue, or slip in a song to play next.

SizeAbout 840 lines of Python in five modules, 45 commits over four years
Librariesdiscord.py, asyncio, yt-dlp, FFmpeg
Commandsjoin leave play add insert remove pause resume previous skip jump queue clear help, most with short aliases
Runs onMy home server set up with a headless Ubuntu Linux install

How it works

  • Streaming, not downloading. yt-dlp looks up a search or URL and returns a direct audio stream, which FFmpeg plays into the voice channel. FFmpeg’s reconnect options keep a stream going through short network drops.
  • Per-server state. The queue, position in the queue, and playback state are stored per server ID, so different groups can use Bobbert at the same time without affecting each other.
  • A queue with history. Songs stay in the queue after they play, and Bobbert tracks his position in it. That’s what makes previous and jump possible.
  • Threading. The voice client calls back from a separate thread when a song ends, so that callback hands the next song back to the main asyncio event loop instead of playing it directly.
  • Housekeeping. Bobbert leaves the channel after 10 minutes of silence or when everyone else leaves, posts embeds with each song’s title and thumbnail, logs errors with timestamps, and has a password-protected reboot command.

Maintenance

Most of what I’ve learned from Bobbert came from keeping him alive. YouTube and Discord change things often, and when they do, something usually breaks. Over four years, I’ve:

  • switched from youtube-dl to yt-dlp when youtube-dl stopped working (2023)
  • updated for breaking changes in discord.py (2023 and 2026)
  • rewritten a large part of the bot after YouTube changed its video formats (2026)
  • fixed queue bugs in insert, remove, and resume as my friends found them

He’s still running today.
