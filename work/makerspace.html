Empty place → Makerspace

Planning, funding, and opening the makerspace at my high school.

When
Dec. 2023 – June 2026
Role
Co-founder, 4-student planning team
Where
Avon Grove High School

Our school district planned to build makerspaces in all four of its schools, starting with the high school. Through the Chief Science Officers program, three other students and I worked with our district’s superintendent and the head of technology to figure out what that space should be, fund it, and set rules for running it.

Design contest (2024)

Rather than decide what went in the room solely by ourselves, we ran a design contest open to students in grades 8–12 and any teachers district-wide. Entrants got the room’s dimensions and a hypothetical budget, and they could submit a 3D model, a drawing, or a written proposal explaining how they’d use the space.

  • I wrote the script for our promo video. The pitch: “It’s just a big empty room. But you can change it.”
  • Our team reviewed the entries and used the best ideas in the plan for the room.

Together, our team secured about $10,000 in grant funding, and the makerspace debuted with an open house in November 2025.

Running it (2026)

Once the room existed, we wrote a plan for how students would actually use it:

  • A makerspace club, starting in 2026–27 and open to anyone, with a two-level certification modeled on our robotics team’s. Level 1 lets a student use equipment with a teacher present. Level 2 lets them train other students.
  • Two planned courses: an intro course on the tools, and an application-based capstone where students spend a year solving one problem and pitch the result to a committee.
  • A student committee that decides what equipment to buy and which projects the club takes on.

When I built CESyL, I had to go to my local library to use a laser cutter. Now students at my school can do that kind of work on campus.
