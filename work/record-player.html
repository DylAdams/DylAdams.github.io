Record player repair

Repairing my grandparents’ turntable from the 70s.

When
Spring 2023
Model
BSR 2310-X
Links
Repair video

In spring 2023, I asked my grandparents if they happened to have a record player I could use before I went and bought a new one. They offered me a 1970s BSR turntable that they dug up from their basement, but it was in rough condition, so I repaired it and documented the process on my YouTube channel.

Initial condition

The power cable, the RCA cables, and the stylus were all rusted, and records skipped constantly. The platter was also slow to start, though it ran normally after a push.

The repair

  1. Cables. I soaked the power and RCA cables in vinegar to remove the rust.
  2. Stylus. I ordered a replacement stylus, but the BSR’s arm head uses a proprietary (or at least non-standard) screw spacing for mounting it. I designed and 3D printed a custom arm head to fit the new stylus, then moved the signal wires over from the old one.
  3. Calibration. I adjusted the arm’s spring tension so the stylus would neither skate across the record nor dig into the groove.

With the new stylus installed and calibrated, the skipping stopped and the turntable played reliably.

What I learned

This project was my introduction to audio hardware, from RCA connections to the full signal chain and why each stage is needed:

StylusConverts the motion of the record’s groove into a very small electrical signal.→ RCA cables
PreampBoosts that signal to line level, the standard level other audio gear expects.→ line level
AmpProvides the power needed to drive a speaker.→ speaker wire
SpeakersPassive speakers need an external amp; powered speakers have one built in, and some include a preamp as well.→ sound

It was an eye-opening introduction to the audio world, and an interest I’ve kept building on since.

Troubleshooting a buzz

Later, I realized a loud buzz had been playing over every record. I isolated it by removing components from the signal chain one at a time, and traced it to a poor ground connection in the wires at the stylus head. Reseating those wires eliminated the buzz.

Today

The turntable is in regular use in my basement workspace, and my collection has grown from a few records to several dozen albums. This project's results have seen constant use since I finished the repair. I love listening to albums on vinyl, and value how vinyl encourages listening to an album as a whole rather than promoting the shuffling of individual songs on streaming services.
